title: "AI speed without users is Waterfall, only faster"
description: "AI lets a team build in hours what used to take a Sprint. If nobody uses those updates until the Sprint Review, the team has rebuilt Waterfall with a faster engine."

---

A team with good AI tooling can now build in an afternoon what used to fill a two-week Sprint. That sounds like pure gain. It is, as long as one thing keeps up with the building: real people using what was built.

## Every update is a guess

Until a user touches it, an update is a guess about what users need. Some guesses are right. Some are wrong. You cannot tell which from inside the team.

Waterfall failed for exactly this reason. Teams stacked months of guesses on top of each other and found out at the end which of the early ones were wrong. By then, everything built later was resting on them.

## AI raises the stacking speed

Scrum shortened the stack. A Sprint is at most a month, and the Sprint Review puts the result in front of stakeholders.

Now look at what AI does to a team that still releases only at the end of the Sprint. Before AI, that team might finish five updates in a Sprint. With AI it finishes twenty-five. If the third one rests on a wrong guess, the old team has two more updates sitting on the mistake. The new team has twenty-two.

Same Sprint length, same Sprint Review, far more rework. The calendar says Scrum. The pile of unchecked assumptions says Waterfall.

## The Scrum Guide already has the answer

> "However, an Increment may be delivered to stakeholders prior to the end of the Sprint. The Sprint Review should never be considered a gate to releasing value."
> *Scrum Guide 2020, Increment*

So the fix needs no change to Scrum. It needs someone to insist on it. That is why the addendum asks the Scrum Master to keep pushing one question in front of the Developers: who has used this yet?

When every update reaches users soon after it is done, a wrong guess costs one update instead of twenty-two. And the faster AI builds, the more that habit is worth.

## What to try next Sprint

1. Pick one update and get it in front of a real user the day it meets your Definition of Done.
2. Write down what you learned before starting the next update.
3. At the Sprint Review, count how many updates were used by someone before the review. Try to raise that number each Sprint.
